Three ways to dress the board

One insignia set, drawn once as SVG symbols. Every style below is the same geometry with a different tile, palette and finish, switched by CSS tokens. Pick one and the art pass builds the full set of 15 ranks, both teams, the board, and the interface in that language.

Each stage shows a 5×3 corner of the 9×8 board mid-game (a Private is selected, with its legal moves and a challenge target), then the same seven ranks for one side, and how the other side's pieces look revealed and hidden.

Recommended

1 · Flat Tactical

Saturated team tiles with filled insignia in brass and ivory, a hard offset shadow, and a light warm-grey board with a thin grid. Reads like a confident modern board-game app.

Highest legibility at phone sizes: filled shapes survive being 40 px tall, and the two team hues never depend on the board color for contrast.

Effort
Lowest. No gradients or filters beyond one shadow.
Mood
Friendly, decisive, daytime.
Teams
Navy vs crimson. Ivory vs charcoal also works if you prefer the classic set.
Risk
Generic if the interface chrome is not given the same attention as the pieces.

2 · Soft Tabletop

Ivory and charcoal tokens with a top sheen, a hairline inner edge and a soft shadow, sitting on a deep-green felt. The closest to owning a good physical set.

The most premium of the three and the calmest to look at for a long game. The ivory side needs the dark felt behind it, so the interface stays dark green too.

Effort
Medium. Sheen and shadow per tile, felt texture for the board.
Mood
Club room, unhurried.
Teams
Ivory vs charcoal, in the tradition of the original set.
Risk
Navy insignia on ivory drop below comfortable contrast on very small screens.

3 · Neon Command

Dark slate tiles with thin-line insignia in cyan against coral, each with a faint glow, on a grid that reads like a war-room table. The most dramatic and the most "video game" of the three.

Strong identity, and hidden enemy pieces feel genuinely unknown in the dark. The whole interface would live in this palette, so there is no light mode.

Effort
Medium. Outline insignia need tuning so 1-star and 5-star stay distinct when small.
Mood
Tactical, late night.
Teams
Cyan vs coral. Both hold on slate; neither is the "good" color.
Risk
Thin lines fade under 40 px; the glow can look dated if overdone.

Whatever the choice, production is the same pipeline: the 15 insignia and tile faces become src/assets/sprite.svg, the palette and finish become src/assets/theme.css, and the board renders pieces with <use>. Changing your mind later costs a stylesheet, not a redraw.
